How to how much fabric do i need

Plan the fabric length for equal rectangular pieces such as cushion faces, panels or simple craft parts. Include seam allowances, shrinkage and the supplier’s purchase increment.

Quick answer: Four 40 × 50 cm pieces, 1 cm seams and a 140 cm usable width need 1.04 m, rounded to 1.10 m in 0.10 m increments.

What you need to measure

  • Finished piece width
  • Finished piece length
  • Number of pieces
  • Seam allowance on each edge
  • Usable bolt width
  • Shrinkage allowance
  • Purchase increment
  • Allow 90° piece rotation: choose the option that matches your layout or product instructions.

How to calculate it

Add two seam allowances to each finished piece dimension and divide by one minus the shrinkage fraction. Fit whole pieces across the usable bolt width, round the required rows up, and multiply by cut-piece length. Round the final fabric length up to the purchase increment. Rotation is considered only when explicitly allowed.

What changes the estimate

This is a rectangular cutting layout, not a garment-pattern estimator. Keep rotation off when grain, nap or a directional print prevents it. The model applies the same shrinkage fraction in both directions. Pattern matching, bias cutting and fabric defects require an additional cutting plan.

Worked example

Four 40 × 50 cm pieces, 1 cm seams and a 140 cm usable width need 1.04 m, rounded to 1.10 m in 0.10 m increments.
