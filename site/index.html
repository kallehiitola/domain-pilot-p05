How much fabric do I need?

Plan fabric yardage or meterage for equal rectangular pieces such as cushion covers, napkins, panels, or craft blocks—accounting for seams, usable bolt width, shrinkage, and store cut increments.

Quick answer: US quilting bolt: four 16 × 16 in pillow panels with 0.5 in seams, 5% shrinkage, and 42 in usable width need 0.99 yd, rounded to 1.00 yd. Metric: four 40 × 50 cm pieces, 1 cm seams, and 140 cm usable width need 1.04 m cut length, rounded to 1.10 m in 0.10 m steps.

Standard fabric bolt widths, usable widths, and typical shrinkage

Always measure usable width between selvedge pinholes before cutting—nominal widths include unfinished edges.

Fabric categoryNominal bolt widthTypical usable widthTypical pre-wash shrinkage

How the calculation works

Expand each finished piece dimension by two seam allowances and divide by one minus the shrinkage fraction. Pack whole cut pieces across the usable bolt width (rounding down), calculate required rows (rounding up), multiply by cut-piece length, and round up to your fabric store purchase increment. Optional 90-degree rotation compares both orientations and picks the shorter cut.

What this estimate does not cover

Designed for equal rectangular cuts, not curved garment patterns. Keep 90-degree rotation unchecked for napped fabrics (velvet, corduroy), one-way prints, or strict grainline projects. Pattern centering, plaid matching, and bias binding require extra yardage.
